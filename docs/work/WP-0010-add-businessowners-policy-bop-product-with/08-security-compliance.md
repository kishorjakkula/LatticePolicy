## Findings

| Minor | **S-1 Accepted deviation: no distribution re-check at servicing.** `lifecycle.service.ts:854` (`renewPolicy`), `:1174` (`rewritePolicy`), the endorsement path and `issuePolicy` do not call `assertBusinessownersAuthorization`. Scenario: a producer's Ohio BOP appointment is terminated after bind. An underwriter, or an agent later given a servicing grant, then renews or rewrites through the API. A new term is written with that unappointed producer and agency still on it, and no `BOP_APPOINTMENT_REQUIRED` refusal. Writing business through an unappointed producer is a licensing and appointment compliance problem in real use. A rewrite is effectively new business, so it is the larger gap. In this scope it is contained: the demo grant covers `NewBusiness` only, so agent servicing goes to an underwriter referral (lifecycle test). The data is synthetic, on the `sample-carrier` tenant only. Wizard servicing still rates through `POST /quotes`, which runs the check. | Accepted for the synthetic demo only, consistent with 07. Before any non-demo enablement, run the check at rewrite and renewal, and ideally at endorsement and issue. Treat it as a Blocker for any real-market package. Record it as a follow-up in 13. | Accepted (demo scope); follow-up required |
| Minor | **S-2 BOP is discoverable outside the demo tenant and not labeled demo in the UI.** `product-registry.ts:91-97` lists every installed pack unless a carrier pack declares `enabledProducts`. `coverage.yaml:10,17-18` labels the product "Businessowners Policy (BOP)" and sets `ui.demo: true`, but nothing in the frontend or server reads `ui.demo`. Scenario: on a stack without `enabledProducts`, any tenant sees BOP in the product picker and can save a draft. Rating and binding then fail closed with `STATE_NOT_ELIGIBLE` because no eligibility row exists. So nothing is exposed or written beyond a draft, but a user could take BOP for a real offering. | Recommend "(Demo)" in the label, or rendering `ui.demo`, before any shared or staging demo. Carrier stacks with `enabledProducts` (for example `examples/carrier-pack/config.yaml`) do not list it. | Open, low risk; a person decides |
| Minor | **S-3 Open regulatory items are recorded only in 01.** `01-requirements.md` lists state admission, filed rates/forms, licensed distribution, mandatory endorsements, terrorism offer/disclosure and servicing notice rules as needing carrier/legal confirmation. The pack README, ADR 0004 (`:40-41`) and task note (`:85`) cover filings and legal approval generally. None of them names the terrorism (TRIA) offer/disclosure, mandatory state endorsements or cancellation/non-renewal notice requirements. Scenario: a carrier adopts this pack as a starting point and ships BOP declarations without a terrorism offer/disclosure or the Ohio-specific endorsements and notices. | Add an "Open regulatory items (carrier/legal confirmation required)" list to `products/businessowners/README.md` or the task note, naming these explicitly. Documentation only. | Open; recommend before Gate D |
| Minor | **S-4 Demo issue exercises a person's authority grant under an automation actor.** `businessowners-demo.service.ts:290-301` rates, binds and issues the example with `users.agent` permissions and the agent's `NewBusiness` authority grant (seeded at `:168-170`), while the recorded actor is `automation:…`. It is still automation performing a bind that, outside the demo, needs a person's authority. | Accepted: local/test only (`:43-45`), opt-in, `sample-carrier` only, synthetic, behind the `--issue-example` flag. A distribution-relevant link is then recreated with only `created_by` and metadata markers on the row, and no onboarding audit event.
